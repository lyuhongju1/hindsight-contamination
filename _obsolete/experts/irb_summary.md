# IRB submission summary (draft for JHU Homewood IRB)

*Draft for the PI. The IRB decides the category. This summary requests a **"not human subjects research" (NHSR) determination**, with **exempt category 2** as the fallback. Check current JHU forms and terminology before submitting.*

---

## Title

Expert judgements of detector-upgrade design problems, for a study of hindsight contamination in AI evaluation.

## Investigator

Hong Joo Ryoo, Department of Physics & Astronomy and Philosophy, Johns Hopkins University. Faculty advisor: [name].

## Purpose

The study tests whether language models propose historically adopted detector upgrades because they recall the historical outcome, or because they reason from the physics. To score the models, we need an answer key for each design problem, including invented ("twin") instruments. Domain experts provide that key: their judgement of which upgrade the stated physics favours.

## Procedures

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Participants | ~25–40 adult professional physicists (faculty, staff scientists, postdocs) with detector-design expertise. No vulnerable populations |
| Recruitment | Individual email (draft attached). Participants are not drawn from the PI's own collaborations, where avoidable |
| Task | An online form: ~12–16 written design problems. For each, the participant lists recommended upgrades, rates candidate upgrades, and answers whether the description reminded them of a real instrument. It ends with background questions (subfield, years of experience, collaboration memberships) and a debrief |
| Duration | ~2–2.5 hours, which can be split |
| Compensation | Flat honorarium of [$amount] |
| Deception | **Incomplete disclosure only.** Invitations describe the study as evaluating AI on design tasks, without the specific hypotheses, and some problems are invented. Both are fully explained in the debrief at the end of the form. No false information is given about the participants' own task |

## Why NHSR may apply

- The study collects participants' expert judgements about physical systems (detectors), not information **about the participants**.
- Their answers serve as an answer key and are analysed as properties of the design problems.

**Caveat for the IRB:**

- Two secondary measures do describe participant behaviour: the agreement between expert ratings and historical outcomes, and how often experts recognize disguised instruments.
- The background questions collect professional information.
- If the IRB considers this enough to make the project human-subjects research, the fallback category is **exempt category 2**: survey procedures with adults, minimal risk, and no sensitive information.

## Risks and protections

- **Risk:** minimal. The only plausible harm is professional embarrassment if an individual's ratings were attributed to them.
- **Protections:**
  - responses are stored under panelist IDs;
  - the ID-to-name key is kept separately, used only for honoraria, and deleted after payment and acknowledgements;
  - results are reported only in aggregate;
  - names are acknowledged only with opt-in consent;
  - data are stored on JHU-approved storage.
- **Public release:** the released dataset contains de-identified ratings without background fields that could identify a person. Field and experience are coarsened, and the collaboration memberships are dropped.

## Consent

Online information sheet and click-through consent at the start of the form, covering purpose (general), duration, compensation, voluntariness, withdrawal, confidentiality, data release, and contact details. Debrief at the end.
