A reactor antineutrino monitor sits at a fixed position 10 m from the centre of a 3.0 GW (thermal) power reactor core.
Its sensitive volume is a 0.9 m3 cube, about 96 cm on a side, built from polystyrene scintillator bars of 2 x 5 cm cross-section and 96 cm length, laid in alternating orthogonal layers.
Each bar is read through wavelength-shifting fibres by SiPMs at both ends, and every channel is recorded by a waveform digitizer.
The scintillator is surrounded by an existing 5 cm polyethylene layer inside an existing lead shield.
Antineutrinos are detected by inverse beta decay: a prompt positron signal followed by a delayed signal from the capture of the neutron, which at present is captured mostly on hydrogen.
With the existing shield in place, ambient radioactivity produces single energy deposits above 1 MeV at a rate of 80 Hz per square metre of the scintillator's outer surface, mostly from the 1.46 MeV and 2.61 MeV gamma lines and their Compton continua.
The monitor's goal is to track the reactor's thermal power from day to day, and its fuel composition over the fuel cycle, from the inverse-beta-decay count and the positron energy spectrum.
The positron energy spectrum must be recorded from 1 MeV of deposited energy upward.
The delayed neutron-capture signal must be recorded for at least 30% of the inverse-beta-decay events that occur in the scintillator.
The rate of accidental prompt-delayed coincidences must stay below the rate of detected inverse-beta-decay events.
