# Feasibility q43: ArgoNeuT -> LArIAT, TPB-coated reflector foils lining the field cage (alternative: TPB plates or light guides in front of PMTs outside the drift region)

- Route 1, dielectric surface charging vs a stated drift-field-uniformity requirement: NO. The baseline field cage is already a dielectric board (G10 or FR4) carrying field-shaping strips, so thin foils laid on it add little new dielectric area facing the drift volume. The size of any charge build-up depends on the ion flux, the bulk and surface leakage of the polymer in LAr, and the geometry, and is uncertain by orders of magnitude, so no ×0.5–2 window exists. Foil thickness, mounting position (between or behind strips) and a resistive backing are settings of the item (lesson 4). Operating precedent: LArIAT, and later field-cage-mounted photon detectors in large LAr TPCs.
- Route 2, total light yield or light-collection uniformity: NO. A lined, wavelength-shifting enclosure gives higher and more uniform collection than plates covering a small fraction of the solid angle, so the item wins on this axis. No requirement makes it fail here while the plates pass.
- Route 3, TPB dissolution or flaking vs a stated argon-purity requirement: NO. The mechanism hits every TPB implementation, so the plate survivor fails too. In addition, TPB loss into LAr is small and not electronegative at a level that threatens the purity requirement robustly.
- Route 4, stated geometry fact (e.g. field-cage-to-cryostat gap): NO. Foils are about 0.1–0.3 mm thick and fit inside any gap. A small gap hurts the alternative (PMTs plus plates outside the drift region) more than it hurts the item. A cryostat-size fact would favour the foils.
- Route 5, trigger or timing requirement: NO. Transit-time spread from multiple reflections in a box of about 0.5 m is a few ns. The direct-view geometry with plates already has a spread of about L/c, roughly 2 ns, and both options share the TPB re-emission time and the LAr singlet time (about 6 ns). The two spreads do not separate by the factor of 4 that ×0.5–2 needs, so no robust window. Trigger thresholds are settings of the item.

VERDICT: NO WINDOW. No stated physical requirement makes the foil lining fail robustly in every implementation while the plate or light-guide alternative passes. The plates outside the drift region would pass everything stated, but the foils also pass, and they win on yield and uniformity.
