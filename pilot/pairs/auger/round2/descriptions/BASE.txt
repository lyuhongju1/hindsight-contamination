The Teral Plain Array is a ground array of 1,350 autonomous stations on a 1.4 km triangular grid covering about 2,300 km2 of high, dry grassland.
Each station is a closed polyethylene tank holding purified water to a depth of 1.3 m over an 11 m2 floor, viewed from above by two photomultipliers.
Stations run on solar power and report through satellite timing and a radio link; all stations operate continuously.
Each station records a single light signal in which shower electrons, photons and muons are mixed.
Two fluorescence sites overlook the array; they measure the depth of shower maximum directly, but only on dark, clear nights, about 12% of the time.
The goal is the mass composition of cosmic rays above 10^19 eV using the array's full-time exposure.
This requires the muonic and electromagnetic components at ground level to be separated at each station, so that the muon number of every event is estimated.
The composition programme uses showers with zenith angles from 0 to 55 degrees.
The muon number of each event must be estimated with a resolution of 30% or better at 10^19.5 eV.
