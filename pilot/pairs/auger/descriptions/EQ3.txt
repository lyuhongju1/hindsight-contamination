Mirelle Steppe Array: a cosmic-ray ground array on a windy steppe at 600 m altitude, built to study air showers above 10^19 eV.
It has 900 stand-alone stations on a 1.8 km triangular grid covering about 2,500 km2. Each station is a sealed water-Cherenkov tank of 12 m2 holding 15 m3 of deionised water (1.25 m deep), viewed through a diffusing liner by two large photomultipliers, with solar power, GNSS timing and a cellular data link.
A single fluorescence telescope site with 12 telescopes works on dark, clear nights, about 12% of the time.
Goal: shower-by-shower discrimination between light (proton) and heavy (iron) primaries above 4x10^19 eV from the always-on ground array, for zenith angles below 50 degrees.
The tank signal is one number per time bin that mixes the electromagnetic component and the muons.
Constraints on any new station hardware:
- tank interiors (water, liner, sensors) must not be opened or changed; the existing calibration and data set must stay valid;
- full tanks (about 15 t) must not be lifted, drained or relocated;
- budget: US$ 8.1 M (US$ 9,000 per station), including installation;
- labour: 4,140 person-hours in total (4.6 per station), with crews driving pickups between stations.
