Corvel Ridge Array: a ground array for cosmic-ray air showers above 10^19 eV on the Tarsa plateau, at 1,050 m altitude, on dry grazing land.
It has 1,200 autonomous stations on a 1.6 km triangular grid covering about 2,650 km2. Each station is a sealed cylindrical water-Cherenkov tank of 11 m2 area holding 14 m3 of filtered water (1.3 m deep) inside a reflective bag, viewed by three photomultipliers, with solar power, satellite timing and a radio link.
Two optical stations of 9 telescopes each record air-fluorescence light on dark nights, about 13% of the time.
Physics goal: classify each shower above 3x10^19 eV as light or heavy (proton versus iron) with the ground array, which runs continuously, for zenith angles below 55 degrees.
Each tank gives one integrated signal in which electromagnetic particles and muons are mixed.
Requirements for any hardware added to the array:
- the tanks are sealed and may not be opened or modified inside; their calibration must match the existing data set;
- the filled tanks (about 14 t) rest on the ground and may not be lifted, drained or moved;
- funds: US$ 9.0 M for hardware and installation (US$ 7,500 per station);
- installation labour: 5,760 person-hours in total (4.8 per station), using light vehicles on unpaved roads.
