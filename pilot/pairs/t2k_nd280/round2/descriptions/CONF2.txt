The Kestrel Flats near station sits 410 m downstream of the production target of an accelerator neutrino beam whose flux peaks near 0.7 GeV.
A warm dipole magnet gives a 0.3 T field over an inner volume about 6.5 m long, and gas drift chambers downstream of the target region measure the momentum and charge of tracks that leave it.
A target region of 1.9 m x 1.8 m x 0.6 m at the upstream end of the magnet is to be re-equipped as an active target in which the neutrino interactions occur and are reconstructed.
The analysis needs the muon and the recoiling hadrons from each interaction, including short proton tracks, at all angles to the beam and not only in the forward direction.
The far detector contains no hydrogen, so interactions on hydrogen nuclei are removed with a model that stays adequate only while hydrogen nuclei make up at most 1% of the target nucleons.
Over the planned run the target medium will absorb a radiation dose of about 5 Gy from beam-related muons and neutrons.
The target region operates at room temperature inside the 0.3 T field.
