The Kestrel Flats near station sits 410 m downstream of the production target of an accelerator neutrino beam whose flux peaks near 0.7 GeV.
A warm dipole magnet gives a 0.15 T field over an inner volume about 6.5 m long, and gas drift chambers downstream of the target region measure the momentum and charge of tracks that leave it.
A target region of 2.6 m x 2.3 m x 0.9 m (the last dimension along the beam) at the upstream end of the magnet is to be re-equipped as an active target in which the neutrino interactions occur and are reconstructed.
The analysis needs the muon from each interaction reconstructed with at least 25% efficiency at every angle to the beam, including tracks perpendicular to it and backward-going tracks.
It also needs the recoiling protons at all angles, including short tracks from protons with kinetic energy down to about 60 MeV.
The far detector contains no hydrogen, so interactions on hydrogen nuclei are removed with a model that stays adequate only while hydrogen nuclei make up at most 2% of the target nucleons.
