The Hollin near detector serves a long-baseline experiment whose beam, after a 290 m decay tunnel, delivers a muon-neutrino spectrum peaking at 0.33 GeV.
The detector occupies a 0.4 T superconducting dipole with an inner volume of 5.0 m x 3.0 m x 3.0 m; scintillator slabs in the yoke tag escaping muons.
At present it has one planar target of 0.8 cm x 2.0 cm polystyrene strips arranged in x and y layers perpendicular to the beam, followed by two gas drift chambers and a lead-plastic calorimeter.
All light is collected by wavelength-shifting fibres onto silicon photomultipliers, and timing is available at the nanosecond level.
The far detector is an imaging water volume with uniform angular acceptance; the cross-section uncertainty propagated to it is 8% and must fall below 4%.
Space of 1.8 m (along beam) x 2.0 m x 0.7 m upstream of the chambers is reserved for a new active neutrino target.
The quantity to maximise is the number of charged-current events in which both the muon and the leading recoil proton are reconstructed.
About 60% of the muons and about 70% of the leading protons are emitted at more than 50 degrees from the beam axis.
The leading-proton momentum distribution has a median of 290 MeV/c, and about 7% of the protons exceed 580 MeV/c.
Interactions on any nucleus are usable in the analysis.
