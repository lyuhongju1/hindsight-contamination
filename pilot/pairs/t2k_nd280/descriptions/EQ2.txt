Detector hall B of the Tervo Valley neutrino facility hosts an off-axis near detector 520 m from the target, sampling a narrow-band muon-neutrino flux that peaks at 0.40 GeV.
The detector is housed in a 0.25 T dipole magnet with a 7.5 m x 3.2 m x 3.4 m inner volume and scintillator counters between the return-yoke plates.
Its existing tracking section has three gas tracking chambers and two segmented plastic targets of 1.0 cm square polystyrene bars, each plane oriented transverse to the beam.
Calorimeter modules of plastic and lead surround the tracker on four sides.
The near detector feeds a joint fit for a distant water far detector; the interaction-model part of the error budget is 5.5% and must drop below 3%.
An upstream section of 2.4 m x 2.2 m x 0.5 m (the 0.5 m vertical) will be emptied and re-equipped as a new active target.
The new target is judged by its yield of charged-current events with both the muon and the leading recoil proton reconstructed, used for transverse-momentum-imbalance analyses.
In this beam about 60% of the muons and about 60% of the leading protons are emitted at more than 50 degrees from the beam direction.
The leading-proton momentum distribution has a median of 320 MeV/c, and about 5% of the protons exceed 640 MeV/c.
Any target nucleus is acceptable for these measurements.
