## BASELINE q30 1504036: The IceCube Neutrino Observatory: Instrumentation and Online Systems (2016-12-15)
Program: IceCube Upgrade (the Gen2 "Phase 1").
items:
 1. mDOM: a multi-PMT module (24 x 3-inch PMTs) in the physics region | commitment 2019-08 (arXiv:1908.09441) | gap 32 | alternative not adopted: the WOM wavelength-shifting optical module, which uses a wavelength-shifting tube read out by small PMTs (Gen2 R&D, PoS(ICRC2017), in arXiv:1710.01207; deployed only as prototypes); also a single 10-inch PMT module, as in PDOM (calibration region only) | conflict idea: borehole diameter and glass/K-40 dark-noise rate against photocathode area and directionality
 2. D-Egg: two 8-inch high-QE PMTs in an elongated glass housing | commitment 2019-08 (arXiv:1908.09441) | gap 32 | alternative not adopted: single 10-inch PMT DOM/PDOM (arXiv:1908.09441; arXiv:1908.11564) | conflict idea: drill-hole diameter against UV transmission of the glass and photocathode area
verdict: ELIGIBLE (items 1, 2), with a dating risk. mDOM and D-Egg appeared from 2015 (ICRC2015, arXiv:1510.05228; ICRC2017, arXiv:1710.01207) as Gen2 R&D designs. The 2017 Gen2 overview calls them "new optical module designs currently being evaluated", so they were options, not commitments (A1 addendum 3). If a reviewer counted the 2015 or 2017 "proposed module" papers as commitments, the gap would be negative or 7 months, and I2 would FAIL. The physical alternatives are weak here: both items vary PMT count and size, not the sensor physics. WOM is the only genuinely different physical approach. Rejected items: the IceTop scintillator panels (2017-07, 7 months, fails I2) and the Gen2 radio array (proposed 2014, before the baseline, fails I2).

## BASELINE q34 1088706: The EXO-200 detector, part I: Detector design and construction (2012-02)
Program: nEXO.
items:
 1. VUV-sensitive SiPM barrel array for LXe scintillation (replacing LAAPDs) | commitment 2017-10 (arXiv:1710.05075, nEXO collaboration) | gap 68 | alternative not adopted: LAAPDs as in EXO-200 (arXiv:1202.2192) or VUV PMTs (Hamamatsu R11410, used in LUX and XENON) | conflict idea: radioactivity per unit photosensitive area, and PDE at 175 nm in LXe against the 1% sigma/Q energy-resolution target
 2. charge readout by crossed strips on 10x10 cm2 dielectric tiles (replacing the EXO-200 wire grids) | commitment 2017-10 (arXiv:1710.05075) | gap 68 | alternative not adopted: wire-plane readout (EXO-200, arXiv:1202.2192) | conflict idea: electrode capacitance and noise per channel, and radiopurity of the substrate. This is closer to readout, so it is the lower preference
verdict: ELIGIBLE (items 1, 2; item 1 preferred). Possible earlier date for item 1: Ostrovskiy et al. 2015 (arXiv:1502.07837), "Characterization of SiPMs for nEXO". I treated it as R&D only. Either date passes I2 and I6. Item 2's wording ("envisioned") is plan-like. Item 1's wording is declarative.

## BASELINE q37 895584: ANTARES: the first undersea neutrino telescope (2011-04)
Candidate program: KM3NeT (main item: the multi-PMT DOM with 31 x 3-inch PMTs replacing the single 10-inch PMT OM; candidate doc: KM3NeT 2.0 LoI, arXiv:1601.07459, 2016-01).
verdict: NOT ELIGIBLE (item definition / successor rule, and I2). ANTARES had no upgrade program of its own. KM3NeT is a separate collaboration, formed by the ANTARES, NEMO and NESTOR communities. It is not a "declared successor detector of the same collaboration", the same situation as CTA for MAGIC. Separately, the multi-PMT DOM was KM3NeT's public design before or close to the baseline: the KM3NeT TDR (2010, ISBN 978-90-6488-033-9, no arXiv) and the TIPP2011 KM3NeT talk (ecap.nat.fau.de, 19+12 3-inch PMT layout). So the first public commitment very likely predates the baseline or falls within 24 months, and I2 fails. Citing the 2016 LoI would not be the first commitment. No claims submitted.

## BASELINE q38 648096: Commissioning and first tests of the MAGIC telescope (2004)
Program: MAGIC-II, the second telescope of the same collaboration.
items:
 1. Increased-QE Hamamatsu R10408 6-dynode PMTs with hemispherical photocathode (about 34% peak QE) in a uniform camera | commitment 2007-07 (arXiv:0709.2474; verifier dates 2007-09) | gap about 31 to 44 (depends on reading "2004" as Jan or Dec) | alternative not adopted: GaAsP hybrid photodetectors, HPD R9792U-40 (about 50% QE). They were planned for a "second phase" (arXiv:0709.2474; arXiv:0907.0865) but never installed in the MAGIC-II camera (arXiv:0906.5448) | conflict idea: night-sky/moonlight photocurrent and ageing or ion feedback in the photocathode, against the gain in QE
 2. Cold-slumped thin-glass mirrors, used alongside 1 m2 diamond-milled aluminium sandwich mirrors | commitment 2007-07 (arXiv:0709.1372; verifier 2007-09) | gap about 31 to 44 | alternative not adopted: thick ground and polished glass mirrors as in H.E.S.S. (Bernlöhr et al., Astropart. Phys. 20 (2003) 111), and MAGIC-I-style thick milled aluminium slabs (arXiv:0709.1372) | conflict idea: areal mass for a fast-slewing light structure (GRB repointing) and freeze-thaw water ingress into the sandwich
verdict: ELIGIBLE (items 1, 2). Caveats: the baseline date is year-only, so the worst-case gap is about 31 months. The HPD in item 1 is "planned for a second phase", so the alternative was deferred, not rejected outright. In practice it was never adopted. CTA is out of scope by the successor rule. Rejected items: the MAGIC-II DRS2/DRS4 2 GSps readout and the sum trigger, both readout or DAQ.
