A liquid-scintillator neutrino detector sits in an underground laboratory at about 3100 m water equivalent. Its target is linear alkylbenzene scintillator with a fluor, filling a 4.6 m-radius transparent membrane vessel. An inert buffer liquid surrounds the vessel inside a 7.2 m-radius steel sphere that carries 2500 photomultipliers. A water Cherenkov muon veto encloses the sphere.
The scintillator solvent is ordinary alkylbenzene with natural hydrogen, about 6e28 hydrogen atoms and 2.9e29 electrons per cubic metre.
The light yield is 450 photoelectrons per MeV, the energy resolution 5.5% at 1 MeV, the vertex resolution 14 cm at 1 MeV, and the analysis threshold 0.25 MeV. Uranium and thorium contamination is below 1e-17 g/g. In the 0.25-0.6 MeV window, the fiducial volume (radius 3.4 m, 165 m3) records about 100 background events per day, mostly from solar neutrinos.
The physics programme is low-energy solar neutrinos (7Be, pep, CNO and 8B), with event-by-event neutron detection by capture gammas.
A shielded access chamber, 1 m across, lies beneath the detector with its centre 7.5 m from the detector centre. It holds up to 2.5 t of emitter plus shielding.
The collaboration now wants to test for oscillation into a sterile state with a mass splitting near 1 eV2, using an intense artificial neutrino emitter placed in that chamber.
The test requires at least 1500 source-induced interactions in the fiducial volume within 18 months of data.
For at least 80% of those interactions, the neutrino energy must be known to within 10%, so that the oscillation pattern can be traced in distance over energy.
Over the counting period chosen for the analysis, source-induced interactions in the selected energy window must outnumber background events in that window.
The target liquid stays as it is, because the solar-neutrino programme continues.
