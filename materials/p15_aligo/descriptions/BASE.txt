A ground-based gravitational-wave detector is a dual-recycled Michelson interferometer with 3.5 km Fabry-Perot arm cavities and a 1064 nm laser.
The arm cavities store about 300 kW; the 60 kg fused-silica test masses hang from four-stage pendulum suspensions on actively isolated in-vacuum platforms.
Frequency-independent squeezed vacuum is injected at the antisymmetric port and a filter cavity for frequency-dependent squeezing is being installed.
The signal is read with DC readout: a differential arm offset of 8 pm places 12 mW of carrier at the antisymmetric port; this offset also couples laser intensity noise and radiation-pressure torque into the readout.
A mismatch of round-trip loss between the two arms puts a further 0.1 mW of carrier, in quadrature with the signal, at the antisymmetric port at zero offset.
Every optic in the vacuum system, including all optics beyond the antisymmetric port, hangs from at least a three-stage pendulum suspension on an actively isolated platform.
The relative motion between any two of these optics along the beam is below 2e-15 m per root hertz at 10 Hz, falling steeply above.
Laser relative intensity noise reaching the antisymmetric port is 3e-10 per root hertz at 10 Hz.
The detector must reach a displacement sensitivity of 4e-19 m per root hertz at 10 Hz and stay quantum-noise limited above 50 Hz, with each technical noise at least a factor of ten below the target across the band.
