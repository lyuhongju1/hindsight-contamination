An underground neutrino detector sits 1.6 km below the surface in a mine cavity.
Its target is 520 tonnes of ultrapure light water held in a transparent acrylic sphere of 10 m diameter with a 1 cm wall. The water inside the sphere is kept separate from the shield water outside it.
About 6,800 10-inch photomultiplier tubes on a support frame of 16 m diameter view the sphere. The cavity around the sphere and the tubes is filled with 4,000 tonnes of ultrapure light water, which acts as the shield.
The detector currently records Cherenkov light from 8B solar neutrinos above about 4 MeV. It collects roughly 8 photoelectrons per MeV.
The next programme targets pep and CNO solar neutrinos, geoneutrinos and reactor antineutrinos. It reuses the cavity, the acrylic sphere, the photomultiplier array and the shield water.
The acrylic sphere, the photomultiplier array and the shield water are retained. The sphere sits entirely in the shield water.
On average, 12% of the photons emitted isotropically inside the sphere produce a photoelectron in the array.
The next programme requires at least 400 photoelectrons per MeV of energy deposited inside the sphere.
The sphere wall, counting its own weight, withstands a net vertical load, upward or downward, of at most 40 kg per cubic metre of contained volume, however the sphere is restrained.
