A planned experiment searches for neutrinoless double-beta decay of 76Ge (Q = 2039 keV) with 600 kg of enriched germanium detectors, operated bare in strings, in a laboratory at 3500 m water-equivalent depth.
The strings stand in liquid argon inside a cryostat surrounded by a water tank used as a muon veto; argon scintillation light vetoes events that deposit energy in the argon, and germanium pulse-shape analysis rejects multi-site and surface events.
In a prototype with this array design and argon of atmospheric origin, the 42K contribution at Q, after vetoes and pulse-shape cuts, was measured at 1.2e-5 counts/(keV kg yr).
42K (half-life 12.4 h) is the daughter of 42Ar (half-life about 33 yr) and beta-decays with a 3.5 MeV endpoint, mostly without a coincident gamma; it is born as a positive ion and drifts to the detector surfaces in their electric fields.
The planned instrument uses the prototype's cryostat, argon recirculation system and trap: the radon in the argon is emanated by the cryostat walls and the argon recirculation system, outside the array; the argon is recirculated through a cold charcoal trap that retains essentially all radon entering it, with the full argon inventory passing through it once every 20 days (radon-222 half-life 3.8 days). Radon is dissolved throughout the argon, and its progeny are born as positive ions and collect on the detector surfaces like 42K.
In the same prototype, the contribution at Q from these radon progeny on the detector surfaces (mainly 214Bi, beta endpoint 3.27 MeV), after the same vetoes and pulse-shape cuts, was measured at 9e-6 counts/(keV kg yr) (radon-progeny gammas are included in the other contributions).
All other contributions at Q are estimated at 1.6e-5 counts/(keV kg yr).
The instrument's goal is a total background index at Q below 2e-5 counts/(keV kg yr), of which at most 4e-6 counts/(keV kg yr) is allotted to beta decays on or near the detector surfaces.
