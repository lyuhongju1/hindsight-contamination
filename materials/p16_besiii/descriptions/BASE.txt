A general-purpose spectrometer runs at an electron-positron collider at centre-of-mass energies of 3 to 5 GeV, with a luminosity of 5e34 cm^-2 s^-1. Its programme is charm, tau and charmonium physics, and it identifies charged pions and kaons up to 1.2 GeV/c by time of flight over a 1.5 m flight path.
Inside a 1.2 T superconducting solenoid sit a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a timing system and a calorimeter of fast undoped CsI crystals.
The barrel timing system meets its goals. Each endcap timing system is one ring of 36 plastic scintillator counters, each about 40 cm long with 600 cm^2 of face area, read out at the outer end by fine-mesh photomultipliers working in the field.
The endcap system measures about 150 ps for pions and worse for electrons, because particles shower in the tracker endplate and support material in front of it and the secondaries reach the same counter.
At the endcap timing position the charged-particle flux, mostly low-energy electrons and positrons from beam-induced background, is between 200 and 400 Hz/cm^2 across the endcap acceptance; over ten years of running this gives an ionizing dose of 10 Gy and a 1-MeV-neutron-equivalent fluence of 1e9 cm^-2.
The tracker and the calorimeter are specified for this background and work at it.
Requirements:
- Every charged track in the endcap acceptance is timed to 60 ps or better, including tracks accompanied by secondaries from upstream material.
- The timing efficiency is at least 95% per charged track at the flux above, everywhere in the endcap acceptance.
- Everything in the endcap region works in the 1.2 T field and at the dose and fluence above.
