A general-purpose spectrometer runs at an electron-positron collider at centre-of-mass energies of about 2 to 4.6 GeV, with a design luminosity of 1e33 cm^-2 s^-1. Its physics programme covers charmonium, open charm, tau and light-hadron spectroscopy, and relies on charged-hadron identification up to about 1 GeV/c.
Inside a superconducting solenoid of about 1 T sit a helium-based small-cell drift chamber, a time-of-flight system and a CsI(Tl) crystal calorimeter; the muon system is in the flux-return yoke.
The barrel time-of-flight is two layers of plastic scintillator bars. Each endcap time-of-flight is one ring of 48 trapezoidal plastic scintillator counters read out by fine-mesh photomultipliers working in the field.
The endcap system was designed for about 110 ps but measures about 140 ps for pions and worse for electrons, because particles shower in the drift-chamber endplate and other material in front of it and the secondaries reach the same counter.
Beam-related charged-particle rates at the endcap timing position are low, of order 100 Hz/cm^2 or less.
The collaboration wants to improve particle identification in the endcap region.
Requirements:
- Every charged track in the endcap acceptance is timed to 80 ps or better, including tracks accompanied by secondaries from upstream material.
- The timing efficiency is at least 95% per charged track everywhere in the endcap acceptance.
- Everything in the endcap region works in the existing solenoid field and fits the existing space between the drift chamber and the endcap calorimeter.
