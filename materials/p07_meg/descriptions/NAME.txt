MEG at the Paul Scherrer Institute searches for the lepton-flavour-violating decay mu+ -> e+ gamma, whose signature is a 52.8 MeV positron and a 52.8 MeV photon emitted back to back and in time.
A continuous surface mu+ beam of about 28 MeV/c stops roughly 3e7 mu+/s in a thin slanted polyethylene target at the centre of a superconducting solenoid. The solenoid field is about 1.27 T at the target and falls toward both ends of the bore.
The positron is measured by low-mass drift chambers inside the magnet and by scintillator timing counters. The photon is measured by a liquid-xenon scintillation calorimeter of about 900 litres, read out by photomultipliers.
The analysis uses the positron energy, the photon energy, the relative angle and the relative time.
The dominant background is accidental: a positron from ordinary muon decay coincides with an unrelated high-energy photon. About half of these photons come from radiative muon decays in the target. Most of the rest come from positron annihilation in flight in detector material.
The accidental background grows as the square of the stopped-muon rate. It sets the sensitivity.
The next phase must reduce the expected accidental background in the signal region by at least 10% at the same stopped-muon rate.
Any change may lower the signal efficiency by at most 2%.
