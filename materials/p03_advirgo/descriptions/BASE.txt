A ground-based laser-interferometric gravitational-wave detector: a dual-recycled Michelson interferometer with 3.6 km Fabry-Perot arm cavities, operating at 1064 nm.
The test masses are 55 kg fused-silica mirrors on monolithic suspensions; the main interferometer topology, arm power and test-mass mass are fixed as stated.
At the planned arm power, radiation-pressure noise equals shot noise at 55 Hz; below this frequency quantum noise is radiation-pressure dominated, above it shot-noise dominated.
A squeezed-vacuum source delivers 13 dB of quadrature noise reduction at its output; the total optical efficiency from the source, through the interferometer, to the output photodetector is 90%.
The longest straight evacuated beam path available for auxiliary optics at the vertex station is 400 m; no longer path exists or can be added at the site, and no auxiliary optics or separate auxiliary beam lines can be installed in the 3.6 km arm tubes.
The best attainable loss of any high-reflectivity mirror at this wavelength is 5 ppm per reflection (absorption plus scatter).
Requirement: at the stated arm power and test-mass mass, the next phase must lower total quantum noise by at least 3 dB at 20 Hz and by at least 3 dB at 800 Hz, relative to the quantum noise of the interferometer with ordinary vacuum entering its output port.
