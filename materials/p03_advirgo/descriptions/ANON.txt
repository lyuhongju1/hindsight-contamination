A ground-based laser-interferometric gravitational-wave detector: a dual-recycled Michelson interferometer with 3 km Fabry-Perot arm cavities, operating at 1064 nm.
The test masses are ~42 kg fused-silica mirrors on monolithic suspensions hung from multi-stage seismic isolators.
At the planned arm power the radiation-pressure and shot-noise contributions to quantum noise cross in the few-tens-of-Hz region, inside the detection band.
A squeezed-vacuum source (more than 10 dB of measured quadrature noise reduction at the source) injects into the dark port; the total efficiency from source to output photodetector is a few tens of percent loss.
The site has long evacuated beam tubes in which auxiliary optics up to several hundred metres long can be housed.
Requirement: at the planned arm power, the next phase must lower total quantum noise by at least 3 dB at every frequency from 20 Hz to 800 Hz, relative to the quantum noise of the interferometer with ordinary vacuum entering its output port.
