ICARUS T600 is a liquid-argon time projection chamber of about 760 t of argon (about 476 t active) in two identical modules, built and operated deep underground at Gran Sasso.
Each module holds two drift volumes that share a central cathode; the drift length is 1.5 m at 500 V/cm, giving a maximum drift time of about 1 ms.
Ionization electrons are read by three planes of wires at 3 mm pitch (two induction planes and one collection plane), about 53,000 wires in total, some up to 9 m long.
Scintillation light is detected by photomultipliers behind the wire planes and provides the trigger and event time.
Wire signals leave the cryostat on cables through flanges in the vessel roof, where the readout crates are mounted; the existing readout digitizes at 10 bits with 400 ns sampling per channel.
The detector is to be refurbished and moved to a near-surface site, under about 3 m of concrete overburden, 600 m from the target of a 1 GeV-scale accelerator neutrino beam, for a short-baseline oscillation search.
At this site about ten cosmic-ray muons cross the detector during each 1 ms drift window.
Requirement: every wire must be recorded continuously over the full drift time for each triggered event.
Requirement: minimum-ionizing tracks must be reconstructed on all three planes, including the induction planes, with a signal-to-noise ratio of at least 10.
Requirement: the readout must sustain the trigger rate of beam spills plus cosmic-ray triggers at the surface without dead time above a few percent.
